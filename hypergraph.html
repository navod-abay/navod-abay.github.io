Hypergraphs

A hypergraph H = (V, E) generalizes a graph by allowing each hyperedge e ∈ E to connect any subset of vertices (|e| ≥ 2), not just a pair. Ordinary graphs are the special case where every hyperedge has size 2.

Incidence Matrix

A hypergraph with n vertices and m hyperedges is represented by an n × m incidence matrix H, where Hij = 1 if vertex i belongs to hyperedge j, and 0 otherwise. Row sums give vertex degrees; column sums give hyperedge cardinalities — the foundation for hypergraph Laplacians and spectral signal processing built on top of H.

Weighted Degrees

Each hyperedge e carries a weight w(e) (default 1, editable in the "Hyperedge Weights" panel). The weighted vertex degree matrix Dv is diagonal with Dv(i,i) = Σe w(e)·Hie. The edge degree matrix De is diagonal with De(e,e) = |e| (its cardinality, unweighted).

Hypergraph Laplacian

Following Zhou, Huang & Schölkopf (2006), define the weighted adjacency-like operator A = H W De−1 HT, where W = diag(w(e)). The (combinatorial) hypergraph Laplacian is:

\[L = D_v - H\,W\,D_e^{-1}\,H^T\]

Normalized Hypergraph Laplacian

Symmetrically normalizing by Dv−1/2 gives the operator Θ = Dv−1/2 H W De−1 HT Dv−1/2 and the normalized hypergraph Laplacian:

\[\Delta = I - \Theta = I - D_v^{-1/2} H\,W\,D_e^{-1}\,H^T D_v^{-1/2}\]

Hypergraph Shift Operator

As in graph signal processing, a shift operator propagates a signal one hop across the structure. Here it's the random-walk operator built from the same weighted adjacency term:

\[S = D_v^{-1}\,H\,W\,D_e^{-1}\,H^T\]

S is similar to Θ (S = Dv−1/2 Θ Dv1/2), so it shares Θ's eigenvalues — the hypergraph's spectral "frequencies" that a filter would act on next.

Spectral Properties

The eigenvalues of L or Δ play the role of graph "frequencies": the smallest (0 for L, since L's rows sum to 0) corresponds to the all-constant signal, and larger eigenvalues correspond to signals that vary more sharply across hyperedges. Because L and Δ are symmetric, both have real eigenvalues and an orthonormal eigenvector basis — the "Spectral Properties" panel diagonalizes whichever operator is selected and lists them.

Spectral Filtering

With eigenvectors V = [v1,…,vn] and a node-domain signal x, the graph Fourier transform x̂ = V−1x re-expresses x in the frequency basis of the selected operator. A filter is any gain g(λi) applied per frequency:

\[x' = V\,\mathrm{diag}(g(\lambda_1),\ldots,g(\lambda_n))\,V^{-1}x\]

g ≡ 1 leaves x unchanged; zeroing gains at large λ is a low-pass filter that smooths x across hyperedges, and zeroing gains at small λ is a high-pass filter that keeps only sharp variation.
